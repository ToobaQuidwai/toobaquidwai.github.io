About Me

Short biography, international scientific journey, and the major themes that organize my research program.

I am an academic cell biologist working across cilia biology, advanced microscopy, membrane trafficking, and disease mechanisms. My research has moved from protein biochemistry and signaling in India and Germany to ciliary transport, super-resolution imaging, and kidney disease biology in the United Kingdom and the United States.

During my PhD at the University of Edinburgh, I identified a coatomer-like role for WDR35/IFT121 in ciliary membrane cargo transport. My postdoctoral work at UMass Chan Medical School expanded that framework into kidney cystogenesis, ADAMTS9-mediated TMEM67 biology, central apparatus function, and Chlamydomonas model systems, while earlier work at EMBL Heidelberg and the Max Planck Institute built my foundation in super-resolution imaging, signaling, and quantitative cell biology.

International research journey

  • India: protein biochemistry and molecular cell biology foundations.
  • Germany: Ras signaling, STORM, PALM, and live-cell super-resolution development.
  • United Kingdom: ciliary transport, electron microscopy, mouse genetics, and disease mechanisms.
  • United States: kidney cystogenesis, TMEM67 biology, and Chlamydomonas-centered postdoctoral research.

Research themes

Theme 01

Cilia Biology

I study how cilia assemble, traffic membrane proteins, and maintain specialized architecture required for signaling and tissue function.

Theme 02

Advanced Imaging

My work uses STORM, PALM, CLEM, 3D-TEM, Airyscan, FRAP, FRET, and FLIM to connect localization, ultrastructure, and mechanism.

Theme 03

Disease Mechanisms

I focus on kidney disease, ciliopathies, metabolic disorders, and other pathologies where defects in ciliary structure and function become biologically consequential.

Theme 04

Translational & Emerging Research

I am interested in how mechanistic cell biology, multi-omics, and imaging can support translational questions and emerging collaborations in disease-oriented research.
